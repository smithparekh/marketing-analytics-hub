Acquisition Metrics
How efficiently you bring in users and traffic
CAC
Customer Acquisition Cost
Core

The total cost to acquire one new paying customer. Includes all marketing + sales spend: ads, tools, salaries, agency fees.

CAC = Total Marketing & Sales Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired
Spend ₹1,00,000 → get 200 customers → CAC = ₹500
Benchmarks (e-commerce):
Good: <₹400 Avg: ₹400–800 High: >₹800
⚠️ Always compare CAC vs LTV — a high CAC is fine if LTV is 3–5× higher.
CPC
Cost Per Click
Paid

How much you pay for each click on your ad. Primary metric for search (Google) and social (Meta) campaigns. Lower is better, but only if those clicks convert.

CPC = Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Clicks
Spend ₹10,000 → 500 clicks → CPC = ₹20
Google Search: ₹15–40 Meta: ₹5–20
CPM
Cost Per Mille (1000 Impressions)
Awareness

Cost to show your ad 1,000 times. Used for brand awareness campaigns where you want maximum reach rather than direct clicks.

CPM = (Ad Spend ÷ Total Impressions) × 1000
Spend ₹5,000 → 2,00,000 impressions → CPM = ₹25
Display: ₹15–30 Video: ₹40–80
CTR
Click-Through Rate
Engagement

Percentage of people who clicked your ad after seeing it. Measures how compelling your creative/copy is. Low CTR = audience mismatch or weak creative.

CTR = (Total Clicks ÷ Total Impressions) × 100
500 clicks ÷ 25,000 impressions = CTR = 2%
Poor: <1% Avg: 1–2% Good: >3%
CPL
Cost Per Lead
Lead Gen

How much it costs to acquire one lead (someone who shows interest — fills a form, signs up, etc). Mid-funnel metric before they become a customer.

CPL = Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Leads Generated
₹50,000 spend → 250 leads → CPL = ₹200
Organic vs Paid Ratio
Traffic Source Mix
Mix

Ratio of organic (SEO, direct, referral) traffic vs paid traffic. A healthy organic ratio reduces CAC over time. Over-dependence on paid = fragile growth.

Organic % = Organic Sessions ÷ Total Sessions × 100
Healthy: 60%+ organic At risk: <30% organic
Revenue & ROI Metrics
How marketing spend translates to money made
ROAS
Return on Ad Spend
Core

Revenue generated for every ₹1 spent on ads. Most direct measure of campaign efficiency. "4× ROAS" means ₹4 revenue for every ₹1 spent.

ROAS = Revenue from Ads ÷ Ad Spend
Revenue ₹4,00,000 ÷ Spend ₹1,00,000 = ROAS 4.0×
Break-even: ~2× Good: 3–4× Excellent: 5×+
⚠️ ROAS ≠ ROI. ROAS ignores product COGS, fulfilment, and overhead. ROAS of 4× with 50% margins might still lose money.
ROI
Return on Investment
Core

Net profit as a percentage of total investment. More complete than ROAS — factors in all costs. The true profitability measure.

ROI = (Net Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100
Profit ₹60,000 ÷ Spend ₹1,00,000 = ROI = 60%
Poor: <10% Avg: 10–50% Strong: 50%+
LTV / CLV
Lifetime Value / Customer LTV
Strategic

Total revenue expected from a customer over their entire relationship with you. Tells you how much you can afford to spend to acquire them.

LTV = AOV × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan
AOV ₹500 × 4/yr × 3 yrs = LTV = ₹6,000
LTV:CAC ratio is the North Star. Aim for 3:1 minimum.
Danger: LTV:CAC <1× Viable: 1–3× Healthy: 3×+
MER
Marketing Efficiency Ratio
Blended

Blended ROAS across ALL channels (paid + organic + email). Unlike platform ROAS, it gives you the true full-picture return. Increasingly preferred over ROAS in multi-channel brands.

MER = Total Revenue ÷ Total Marketing Spend
Total Revenue ₹50L ÷ Total Spend ₹10L = MER = 5×
AOV
Average Order Value
Revenue

Average amount spent per transaction. Increasing AOV is often cheaper than reducing CAC. Upsell, cross-sell, and bundling all improve AOV.

AOV = Total Revenue ÷ Number of Orders
₹5,00,000 revenue ÷ 1,000 orders = AOV = ₹500
Contribution Margin
After Variable Costs
Profitability

Revenue minus all variable costs (COGS, shipping, payment fees, returns). What's left to cover fixed costs and profit. Often more meaningful than gross margin for DA reporting.

CM = Revenue − (COGS + Variable Costs)
Revenue ₹1,000 − Costs ₹600 = CM = ₹400 (40%)
Conversion Metrics
How well you turn visitors into buyers
CVR / CR
Conversion Rate
Core

Percentage of visitors who complete the desired action (purchase, sign-up, download). The single most important metric to optimize on your site.

CVR = (Conversions ÷ Total Visitors) × 100
500 purchases ÷ 20,000 visitors = CVR = 2.5%
Poor: <1% Average: 1–3% Good: 3%+
Bounce Rate
Single-page Session %
Friction

% of visitors who leave after viewing only one page without any interaction. High bounce = landing page mismatch, slow load, or irrelevant traffic. GA4 uses "engagement rate" now (inverse).

Bounce Rate = Single-page Sessions ÷ All Sessions × 100
8,000 bounces ÷ 20,000 sessions = 40% Bounce Rate
Good: <40% Avg: 40–60% High: >70%
Cart Abandonment Rate
Add-to-Cart → No Purchase
E-commerce

% of shoppers who add items to cart but don't complete purchase. Industry average is 70%+. Reducing this by even 5% can dramatically lift revenue.

CAR = (1 − Purchases ÷ Cart Additions) × 100
100 carts, 30 purchases → 70% Abandonment
Great: <60% Avg: 65–75% High: >80%
Funnel Drop-off
Stage-by-Stage Loss
Funnel

At each funnel stage, what % of users proceed vs drop. Pinpointing the highest drop-off stage is the highest-leverage CRO opportunity.

Drop-off % = (Stage N − Stage N+1) ÷ Stage N × 100
1000 → 200 checkout starts → 80% drop at checkout entry
RPCV
Revenue Per Converting Visitor
Value

Average revenue generated by each visitor who converts. Combines CVR × AOV in a single per-visitor value metric. Useful for paid traffic bid optimization.

RPCV = Total Revenue ÷ Converting Visitors
₹5,00,000 ÷ 500 buyers = RPCV = ₹1,000
Time to Convert
Days to First Purchase
Behaviour

Median number of days from first touch to first purchase. Critical for retargeting window settings and email nurture sequence length. Varies massively by product category.

Avg(Purchase Date − First Session Date)
Impulse: 0–2 days Considered: 7–14 days High-ticket: 30–90 days
Retention Metrics
How well you keep the customers you acquire
Churn Rate
Customer Attrition Rate
Core

% of customers lost in a given period. The silent killer — a 5% monthly churn means losing 46% of customers per year. "Revenue churn" (MRR lost) is often more impactful than customer count churn.

Churn = Lost Customers ÷ Start-of-Period Customers × 100
50 lost ÷ 1000 start = 5% Monthly Churn
SaaS good: <2%/mo E-com: 5–10%/mo normal
Retention Rate
Customer Survival %
Core

% of customers still active after a time period. Inverse of churn. Cohort-level retention curves reveal if your product has long-term value.

Retention = (End Customers − New) ÷ Start Customers × 100
Start: 1,000 · End: 980 · New: 50 → 93% Retention
NPS
Net Promoter Score
Loyalty

Survey-based score measuring customer loyalty. "How likely are you to recommend us?" on a 0–10 scale. Promoters (9–10) minus Detractors (0–6) as a % of respondents.

NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors
50% promoters − 20% detractors = NPS = +30
Poor: <0 OK: 0–30 Good: 30–70
Repeat Purchase Rate
Repurchase / Reorder Rate
E-com

% of customers who buy more than once. High RPR = strong product-market fit and good customer experience. A crucial signal for D2C brand health.

RPR = Repeat Customers ÷ Total Customers × 100
300 repeat ÷ 1000 total = RPR = 30%
New brand: ~10–15% Mature: 25–40%+
CSAT
Customer Satisfaction Score
Satisfaction

Post-interaction satisfaction score (usually 1–5). Measures specific touchpoints (support, delivery, product) unlike NPS which measures overall loyalty.

CSAT = (Satisfied Responses ÷ Total Responses) × 100
400 happy ÷ 500 total = CSAT = 80%
Good: >75% Poor: <60%
Win-back Rate
Lapsed Customer Recovery
Recovery

% of churned/dormant customers successfully reactivated through win-back campaigns. Reactivation is typically 5–10× cheaper than acquiring new customers.

Win-back = Reactivated ÷ Targeted Lapsed × 100
50 reactivated ÷ 500 targeted = 10% Win-back
Engagement Metrics
How users interact with your product and content
DAU / MAU
Daily / Monthly Active Users
Activity

DAU/MAU ratio (stickiness) shows how often users return. 20%+ means strong daily habit. Facebook is 60%+; most apps are 10–20%.

Stickiness = DAU ÷ MAU × 100
5,000 DAU ÷ 50,000 MAU = 10% Stickiness
Low: <10% OK: 10–20% High: 20%+
Session Duration
Avg Time on Site/App
Depth

Average time a user spends per session. Higher duration = deeper engagement, but context matters — a long time on a checkout page means friction, not engagement.

Avg Duration = Total Session Time ÷ Total Sessions
Poor: <1 min Avg: 2–4 min Engaged: 4+ min
Pages per Session
Depth of Browsing
Browse

Average number of pages viewed in a session. High pages/session = exploratory browsing or good internal linking. Low = either found what they needed fast, or bounced.

PPS = Total Pageviews ÷ Total Sessions
60,000 pageviews ÷ 20,000 sessions = 3.0 pages/session
Social Engagement Rate
Interactions per Impression
Social

% of people who interact (like, comment, share, save) with your content. Instagram saves are the highest-quality signal — they signal the content was worth keeping.

ER = (Likes + Comments + Shares) ÷ Impressions × 100
Low: <1% Avg: 1–3% Strong: 3–6%
Email Open Rate
Opens ÷ Delivered
Email

% of delivered emails that were opened. Subject line quality and list hygiene are the main levers. Note: Apple MPP inflates open rates since iOS15 — track clicks instead.

Open Rate = Opens ÷ Delivered Emails × 100
Poor: <15% Avg: 20–25% Good: 30%+
CTOR
Click-to-Open Rate
Email

% of people who clicked after opening. More reliable than raw CTR because it removes list quality as a variable. Measures how well your email body/CTA performs.

CTOR = Unique Clicks ÷ Unique Opens × 100
200 clicks ÷ 1,000 opens = CTOR = 20%
Good: 15–25%
Marketing Funnel
TOFU → MOFU → BOFU — awareness to purchase
The Full Conversion Funnel
Example: 10,000 impressions entering a D2C e-commerce funnel
Funnel Stages Explained
TOFU = Top of Funnel · MOFU = Middle · BOFU = Bottom
TOFU
Awareness / Discovery
Top

First touchpoint. Goal: reach as many relevant people as possible. Metrics: Impressions, CPM, Reach, CTR. Channels: Meta/Google Display, YouTube, Influencer, SEO (informational).

Impression → Click (CTR%) → Website Visit
MOFU
Consideration / Interest
Middle

Visitor is aware, now you need to build intent. Metrics: Pages/session, Time on Site, Lead form submissions, Email sign-ups. Channels: Retargeting, Email nurture, Product pages.

Visit → Engagement → Lead / Wishlist
BOFU
Decision / Purchase
Bottom

High-intent stage. Goal: remove friction and close. Metrics: CVR, Cart Abandonment, AOV, Checkout completion rate. Channels: Search retargeting, abandoned cart email, promo codes.

Cart Add → Checkout → Purchase → Post-purchase
